Home Training Program

Two adjustable dumbbells + rowing machine. Priorities: core strength, larger arms, efficient strength work, frequent rowing, and enough recovery for soccer.

Weekly structure

Four purposeful training days; the other mornings stay easy.

Soccer rule Move the days around your soccer session. Ideally keep the day immediately before soccer light. Avoid placing hard rowing intervals or the sumo-squat session immediately before soccer when possible.
Core strategy No separate plank routine is required initially. Rowing provides repeated trunk stabilization; sumo squats, one-arm rows, RDLs and standing presses add loaded bracing; suitcase carries provide heavy one-sided stabilization that rowing does not.

Progression rule

For a movement prescribed as 3 × 8–12, use a weight that leaves roughly 2 clean repetitions in reserve.

Example: 10/9/8 → 11/10/9 → eventually 12/12/12. Once you can complete the top of the range with good form, increase the dumbbell one increment and build up again.

Do not progressively overload the light morning rows. They are there to make you feel better, not to create fatigue.

First two weeks

  • Week 1: 2 sets per strength exercise; 3 sets for curls/extensions. Do the power row hard, but keep the second rowing session moderate.
  • Week 2: Move compounds to 3 sets; both heavy rowing sessions can follow the full prescription.
  • Week 3 onward: Full program.

Light Rowing

Morning activation, mood and movement — not a performance test.

8–12 MIN
RPE 2–3 / 10
  1. 2 min extremely easy.
  2. 6–8 min comfortable continuous rowing.
  3. 1–2 min extremely easy cooldown.
Correct intensity You can speak normally and step off the rower feeling more awake, not tired. Roughly 18–22 strokes/min is a reasonable guide, but comfort matters more than the number.
Rowing technique

Drive: legs → torso → arms. Recovery: arms → torso → legs.

Heavy Row 1 — Power

Short, forceful strokes. Strength-like rowing without turning it into a long cardio session.

12–15 MIN
8 rounds: 10 powerful strokes + 50–60 sec very easy
  1. Warm-up: 3–4 min easy.
  2. Main set: 8 rounds of 10 powerful strokes, then 50–60 sec very easy — keep rowing slowly during the recovery; do not stop or get off the machine.
  3. Cooldown: 2–3 min easy.
How one round looks Pull 10 hard, forceful strokes → then row at a gentle, almost effortless pace for 50–60 seconds → repeat from the beginning. You stay on the rower the whole time.
Power cue Think “strong stroke, not frantic stroke.” Around 18–22 strokes/min during the power work is fine. Produce force through the legs, transfer it through a braced torso, and finish with the arms.
Do not Set the resistance to maximum and grind. The goal is controlled power and clean technique.

Heavy Row 2 — Intervals

Your harder conditioning session. Short because cardio is not the main limiting goal.

~18 MIN
6 × (45 sec hard + 75 sec very easy)
  1. Warm-up: 3 min easy.
  2. Main set: 6 rounds of 45 sec hard + 75 sec very easy.
  3. Cooldown: 3 min easy.
Hard = about RPE 8/10 Not an all-out sprint. The sixth interval should still look technically similar to the first. A hard-stroke rate around 24–30/min is a reasonable guide.
Technique reminder

Keep the sequence legs → torso → arms. Higher effort should come from more force as well as cadence, not from shortening the stroke into frantic arm pulling.

Program A

Squat · chest · back · dedicated core

25–35 MIN
Efficient pairing Sumo squat → floor press → rest ~90 sec, repeat ×3. Then one-arm row → suitcase carry → rest 60–90 sec, repeat ×3.

1. Dumbbell Sumo Squat

3 × 8–12

Purpose: legs, glutes and loaded trunk bracing with less need for additional squat/lunge volume.

  • Stand wider than shoulder width; toes slightly outward.
  • Hold one dumbbell vertically between the legs; later use more load if needed.
  • Brace the abdomen before descending.
  • Keep knees tracking in the direction of the toes.
  • Use a controlled, comfortable depth. Pain-free movement matters more than maximum depth.
Avoid: knees collapsing inward, bouncing at the bottom, or forcing depth.
YouTube: dumbbell sumo squat technique

2. Dumbbell Floor Press

3 × 8–12

Purpose: chest + triceps + front shoulders.

  • Lie on your back with knees bent and feet planted.
  • Lower the dumbbells under control until the upper arms gently touch the floor.
  • Keep elbows somewhat tucked rather than flared straight sideways.
  • Press upward without bouncing the upper arms off the floor.

3. One-Arm Dumbbell Row

3 × 8–12 each side

Purpose: heavier back and biceps loading than the rowing machine provides.

  • Brace the free hand on a solid chair, sofa or other stable surface.
  • Keep your torso stable and spine neutral.
  • Pull the dumbbell toward the hip/lower ribs.
  • Lower under control and reach naturally at the bottom.
Avoid: rotating the whole torso to throw the dumbbell upward.
YouTube: one-arm dumbbell row technique

4. Suitcase Carry / Hold

3 × 30–45 sec each side

Purpose: heavy one-sided core stabilization and grip. This complements the more repetitive trunk work from rowing.

  • Hold one heavy dumbbell at your side.
  • Stand tall with shoulders level and ribs controlled.
  • Walk slowly if you have space; otherwise stand still.
  • Stay upright as if the dumbbell were not trying to pull you sideways.
Avoid: leaning toward or away from the weight.
YouTube: suitcase carry technique
Finish 5–8 min very easy rowing, RPE 2–3. This is a cooldown, not another hard training block.

Program B

Posterior chain · shoulders · arms

25–35 MIN
Efficient pairing Romanian deadlift → overhead press → rest ~90 sec, repeat ×3. Then hammer curl → triceps extension → rest 60–90 sec, repeat ×4.

1. Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift

3 × 8–12

Purpose: hamstrings, glutes, posterior chain and heavy trunk bracing.

  • Stand holding both dumbbells in front of the thighs.
  • Keep a small bend in the knees.
  • Brace, then push the hips backward.
  • Keep dumbbells close to the legs.
  • Stop when you feel a strong hamstring stretch while maintaining a controlled spine position.
  • Drive the hips forward to stand.
Main cue: “hips backward,” not “squat down” and not “reach the dumbbells to the floor.”

2. Standing Dumbbell Overhead Press

3 × 6–10

Purpose: shoulders + triceps + standing core stabilization.

  • Start with dumbbells at shoulder/ear level.
  • Brace the abdomen and keep the ribs controlled.
  • Press overhead to a comfortable lockout.
  • Lower under control.
Avoid: leaning substantially backward to finish the rep. Reduce the weight if necessary.

3. Dumbbell Hammer Curl

4 × 8–12

Purpose: direct arm-size work for the biceps/brachialis and forearms.

  • Stand tall with palms facing inward.
  • Keep elbows close to the sides.
  • Curl without swinging the torso.
  • Lower slowly and completely under control.

4. Overhead Dumbbell Triceps Extension

4 × 10–15

Purpose: direct triceps work for upper-arm size.

  • Start with one dumbbell held securely with both hands overhead.
  • Lower it behind the head primarily by bending the elbows.
  • Keep the upper arms reasonably stable.
  • Extend the elbows smoothly; do not throw the weight.
